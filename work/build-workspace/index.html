Overview

HP Build Workspace connects digital drawings and printed plans around one reliable source. I helped shape the product around the drawing as a shared workspace, keeping versions, markups, photos, tasks, and decisions together as work moved between the office and job site.

The project started as an exploration of new markets for HP, investigating how its expertise in printing, scanning, and document management could address challenges beyond traditional print production. Construction emerged as an opportunity: teams relied on fragmented tools and paper-based workflows to manage drawings, revisions, and on-site communication. The challenge was to identify a meaningful role for HP in this ecosystem.

As Design Lab Lead, I helped translate that market opportunity into a product vision: make the drawing the shared workspace. We designed around four core workflows: storing and sharing plans, collaborating directly on drawings, reviewing revisions, and reporting progress from the field. Annotations, photos, comments, and tasks remained connected to specific drawing locations, while QR codes bridged printed plans with their digital source.

The key design decision was to keep paper. Early on, site visits showed foremen working from printed sets they trusted more than any tablet, so instead of replacing plans we made them a way in: every printed sheet carries a QR code that opens its latest version, with markups and open tasks pinned in place. That kept HP’s printing at the centre of the product rather than at its edge.

The result was HP Build Workspace, a live product connecting digital and physical construction workflows through a shared project record. Pilots with general contractors ran on active job sites before launch, and their results were what convinced HP to fund it as a new business rather than an experiment.
