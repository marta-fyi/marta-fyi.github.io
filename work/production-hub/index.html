Overview

HP PrintOS is the software large print companies use to run their production. Over the years it had grown into separate apps, and each one kept its own piece of every order.

I rebuilt it around the order. Now one job carries its whole story, from the first request to the final delivery, and every team works from the same place.

As design lead, I identified the core problem with HP PrintOS: production teams were working across disconnected apps, each maintaining its own version of an order. The issue wasn’t inconsistent UI; it was a fragmented product architecture. Rather than redesigning screens, I led a shift to an order-centric platform, establishing one shared data model that connected existing services without requiring a costly, full-scale migration.

I shaped the model around the complexity of real production workflows, from simple print jobs to global campaigns involving hundreds of items across multiple production runs. I defined how orders, items, and runs relate, and designed workflows that accommodate incomplete briefs, approvals, scheduling, reprints, and partial deliveries. Each stage has clear ownership, prerequisites, and rules, making the system flexible enough for real-world operations while keeping changes controlled and traceable.

The result was a live production platform where teams share a consistent view of every job, disruptions propagate across affected orders, and automation is built directly into the workflow. The key design decision was treating the underlying model and workflow as the product, not the interface alone. This required teams to relinquish ownership of isolated data models, but created a coherent foundation for scaling the platform and delivering more reliable production operations.
