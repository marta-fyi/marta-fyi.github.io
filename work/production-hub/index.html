Overview

HP PrintOS had the tools to run a print business, but they lived across separate applications. I reframed the platform around one order that could persist from request to delivery, while each team saw the part of the work it needed.

Long story, short.

A product with good features, but the work broke between them.

Quoting, scheduling, tracking, reporting, and device management became separate applications. Too many options and inconsistent interfaces made the product very complex and difficult to navigate. Scattered information weakened usability, and layered navigation and repetitive manual work slowed teams down.

Churn and growth data showed that the platform experience needed attention.

Approach

One order should remain recognizable from request to delivery.

The unit of design became the production flow rather than the individual application. Files, requirements, approvals, priority, owners, tasks, assigned devices, and status needed to travel together. A shared record creates continuity, and role-specific views make that continuity useful.

Account teams needed a planning view, pre-press teams needed approval and production details, production teams needed capacity and device schedules, and operations teams needed clear ownership and live status.

Assembling the platform

The interface changes with the job. The production record doesn’t.

01 / For Accounts

Start with the full picture. Client work, personal tasks, project progress, queues, and messages share one planning surface.

02 / For Pre-press

Prepare the order with its context intact. Approval, specifications, related tasks, people, and assigned stations remain connected.

03 / For Production

Plan against real capacity. The schedule connects jobs to RIP, printers, cutters, people, and time.

04 / For Operations

Keep ownership and status visible. The board shows what is moving, what is blocked, and who needs to act.

Design approach

Standardize the record, adapt the view, and automate the handoff.

Those three decisions turned a set of applications into a platform model. Each decision addressed a different source of discontinuity.

Standardize what production receives. A configurable order captures the information downstream teams need before work begins. The record—not the form—becomes the shared object.

Let routine changes move the work forward. Rules connect approvals, assignments, deadlines, and task changes to the next useful action without removing visibility from the team.

Connect assignments to production stations. Order details, comments, device requirements, and assigned stations stay together so teams can see capacity conflicts before they become delays.

Shipped outcome

Production Hub shipped as one connected workflow.

The live platform now connects the work, end to end:

  1. Standardized quotes and orders brought client communication, file exchange, tracking, and payments into one flow.
  2. Immediate order status, priorities, and automated rules reduced manual coordination before production.
  3. Capacity planning connected incoming orders, deadlines, device schedules, and floor assignments.
  4. Live task updates, device oversight, and maintenance planning kept office and production-floor work aligned.

About

A platform is a shared model of the work: the objects that persist, the people who act on them, and the transitions the system can carry without forcing teams to reconstruct the process. Mapping out the pattern and wiring the ecosystem was the tricky part, choosing new brand interactions was the reward.
